# Structural entrapment and collapse

Orientation only. Structural collapse work is a technical discipline. If your unit is not
equipped and trained for it, the useful action is to locate, mark, protect and request the
capability — not to start moving debris.

## What a structural-entrapment row implies

`STRUCTURAL_ENTRAPMENT` means the person reported being trapped: by collapse, by debris, or
by a pinned limb. It sorts below drowning because the clock is usually hours rather than
minutes — usually, not always. Entrapment plus water, or entrapment plus a rising level, is a
minutes-scale problem wearing an hours-scale label, and the board cannot know that. A
responder reading both fields can.

## Before anyone approaches

- Assume secondary collapse. What fell once is what is holding the rest up.
- Look for the hazards that kill responders rather than casualties: unsupported walls and
  floors, hanging debris, live electrical service, gas, and voids that can swallow a
  standing person.
- Cut utilities to the structure where that is possible and authorised before entry.
- One way in, one way out, and someone outside who knows how many people are inside.

## Locating people in rubble

- **Hail and listen.** Call, then stop everything and be silent for a set period, then
  repeat. Silence periods are only useful if the whole area actually stops.
- Work the likely survivable spaces: voids beside heavy furniture and appliances, under
  stairs, along the edges of collapsed slabs, and in the lee of anything that resisted the
  load.
- **Mark what you search** so a second team does not repeat it, and record what you found —
  including nothing.

## Release, and why it is not just lifting

Debris on a casualty is often load-bearing. Removing it without support can drop what it was
holding.

- Shoring goes in before the load comes off, done by people trained to place it.
- Lift in small increments and support each increment before taking the next.
- A person pinned for a prolonged period can be seriously harmed by sudden release — the
  medical risks of crush injury are real and are the reason release is planned with medical
  capability present, not performed as fast as possible.

Nothing on this board can tell you whether a release is safe. That judgement is made at the
casualty by someone qualified.

## Evidence flags that recur here

- `pinned` — the device has been effectively motionless in an orientation consistent with
  being trapped. It is a strong flag on this board and it says nothing about what is on top
  of the person.
- `impact` — a shock event was recorded on that device.
- `enclosed / dark` — the light sensor is dark. A phone in a pocket is also dark; this flag
  is weak alone and meaningful alongside `pinned` or `impact`.
- `screaming` — a voice-like spectral match near the device. It is evidence someone is
  conscious near that phone; it may also be a rescuer's voice.

## When the row is unplaced

An `UNPLACED` entrapment is a search task, not a dispatch to a point. The useful board fields
are the hop distance (how near the device has ever reached a node of ours) and the zone tag
if any. Say plainly that the location is unknown; an unplaced casualty drawn confidently in
the wrong place costs a search, which costs someone else their window.
